Share a workspace with your team: invite people by email and give each one a role that decides what they can see and change.

## Invite a teammate

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Settings > Members**.

</Step>

<Step>

Type addresses into **Emails** in the **Invite teammates** box.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick one or more **Roles**, and optionally narrow their **Access**.

</Step>

<Step>

Press **Send invitations**.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- Every workspace starts with **Admin**, **Manager** and **Viewer** roles, which you can edit, delete or add to.
- **Owner** is a membership status, not a role, and there is exactly one per workspace.
- A member with several roles gets the union of all of them.
- A member limited to **Selected resources** has read-only access.
- Inviting, re-roling and removing members needs the **Manage team** permission.

## Where team settings live and who manages them

Share a workspace with your team: invite by email, give each person a role, and the role decides what they can see and change. An optional access scope narrows which campaigns and mailboxes the role applies to, so a client or contractor can work inside your workspace without seeing the rest of it. The roster and invitations live under **Settings > Members**; roles and the permission matrix under **Settings > Roles & access**.

<Callout type="info" title="Who can manage the team">
Inviting, re-roling, removing, and managing roles all need the **Manage team** permission (the owner and the seeded Admin role have it; any custom role can carry it). Without it the roster is read-only.
</Callout>

</Fold>

## Inviting members

In the **Invite teammates** box, paste or type addresses into **Emails**, separated by comma, semicolon, space, or Enter. Each becomes a chip: valid ones grey, malformed ones red so you can remove them first. Pick one or more **Roles** for the batch (a description on the right says what you are granting), optionally narrow the batch's **Access** (see [access scope](#access-scope)), then **Send invitations**. A restricted invitee is restricted from the moment they join; there is no window in which they see the whole workspace.

Warmbly sends one invitation per address and reports results as "Invited 2 · 1 failed". Invalid addresses are skipped and called out. The chips clear when the batch finishes.

When several trusted dashboards share one backend, invitation emails link back to the dashboard that sent the invitation. Copying an invitation link also keeps that dashboard's hostname. See [multi-dashboard configuration](/development/configuration/#google-sign-in-per-dashboard-deployment).

**Pending invitations** shows the email, role, access, and expiry for each unaccepted invite, with two actions on the right of the row: copy the invite link (to share directly instead of waiting on email) and cancel the invitation. There is no resend button. To give somebody a fresh link, invite the same address again: that replaces the invitation in place with a new token and a reset expiry, and the previously emailed and copied links stop working. Copying the link again also issues a new link, so only the most recently copied link works; the emailed link is unaffected.

<Callout type="info" title="Roles are not permanent">
The invite-time role can be changed inline from the roster later, so it is fine to start people narrow and widen access afterward.
</Callout>

### On a self-hosted instance

Two things work differently when you run Warmbly yourself.

**An invited person creates their account from the invitation link.** A self-hosted instance ships with signups closed to the public (`DISABLE_REGISTRATION=invite_only`), so visiting the sign-up form directly is refused. The invitation link carries the token that permits the signup, which is why the flow works: the invitee opens the link, clicks **Create an account**, and lands inside your workspace rather than in a new one.

**Invitations may not arrive by email.** Platform mail defaults to a transport that writes messages to the backend log instead of delivering them, so copying the invite link is the path rather than the fallback. The members page shows an amber notice above the **Emails** field when the server reports that mail is not delivered, telling you to invite the person and then copy the link from their row under **Pending invitations**.

Both are covered in [accounts and access](/development/accounts-and-access/#without-a-mail-relay), along with how to point the instance at a real relay.

## The roster

Each row shows email, role, access, and join date, with your own row tagged "you". Open the role picker on a row to tick the roles that member holds; a member can hold several, and their effective access is the union. The owner's row is a fixed Owner badge, and your own row is not editable.

To remove someone, hover (or tap) their row and click the remove icon. **You cannot remove the owner or yourself.** To leave a workspace you own, transfer ownership first.

Removal takes effect at once, including for someone who is signed in right now. Their open dashboard loses the workspace on its next request and its live updates stop, and they are asked to pick another workspace they belong to. Apps they authorized in the workspace are disconnected. Nothing they created is removed with them: mailboxes, contacts, campaigns and API keys belong to the workspace, so revoke their keys under **API keys** if those should go too. A changed role or a new owner reaches open dashboards' live updates without a reload.

</Fold>

## Access scope

A role says what a member may do. Their access scope says which resources it applies to:

- **Entire workspace**, the default and what every member had before scopes existed.
- **Selected resources**, which reaches only the campaign folders, campaigns and mailboxes you choose.

Click the **Access** cell on a member's row to change it. In the dialog, choose **Selected resources**, then pick from three lists:

| Grant | Gives access to |
| --- | --- |
| **Campaign folders** | Every campaign the folder holds at the time of each request. Moving a campaign into the folder grants it, moving it out takes it away. |
| **Campaigns** | One campaign, for workspaces that do not organize by folder. |
| **Mailboxes** | The conversations in that mailbox in the unified inbox. |

A campaign is reachable when it sits in at least one granted folder or is granted on its own. Campaign access never includes a mailbox, even the ones the campaign sends from: inbox access is always granted mailbox by mailbox. Under the mailbox picker, **Senders of the selected campaigns** lists the mailboxes the chosen campaigns and folders send from, to review and add with one click. A restricted member with nothing selected reaches nothing.

<Callout type="warn" title="Restricted access is read-only">
A restricted member's roles still apply, but only their read permissions do: **View campaigns**, **View analytics** and **Use unified inbox** (reading only). Everything else is set aside while the scope is restricted, so they cannot edit, start, send, reply, label, move, or mark anything read or unread. Opening a conversation does not mark it read for the rest of the team.
</Callout>

What a restricted member sees:

- **Campaigns**: the list, folder counts, each granted campaign's settings, steps and activity feed, and its analytics. Any other campaign answers as not found, including by direct link.
- **Analytics**: the dashboard and campaign reports, computed only over their campaigns and mailboxes.
- **Inbox**: the conversations of their mailboxes, with counts and filters to match.
- **Live updates** for those resources only. They do not appear in the team's presence and do not see who else is online.

Everything that cannot yet be narrowed to a scope is unavailable to them, and shows as locked in their sidebar with the reason: contacts, CRM, templates, forms, automations, deliverability, placement tests, settings other than their own profile and security, the AI assistant, and the Slack app. They are not sent workspace notifications. An API key a restricted member already holds, and an app they authorized, are held to the same scope and the same read-only routes; they cannot create new keys.

Changing a member's access needs **Manage team** and a recent sign-in, takes effect on their next request, and applies to their open dashboard and live updates without a reload. You must hold every permission the member's roles grant, the owner always reaches the entire workspace, and you cannot change your own access. Grants on a deleted folder, campaign or mailbox disappear with it.

</Fold>

## Roles

Roles are workspace data. Every workspace starts with three seeded roles that are ordinary roles in every respect: rename, recolor, re-permission, or delete them, and add your own. **Owner is a membership status, not a role**, and there is exactly one per workspace.

| Role | Starts with |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** (status) | Full control, including ownership transfer |
| **Admin** | Everything except transferring ownership |
| **Manager** | Day-to-day operator: campaigns, contacts, mailboxes, integrations. No team, billing, settings, or API keys |
| **Viewer** | Read-only |

### Permissions

| Area | Capability | Allows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data** | View / manage campaigns | Read settings, sequences, analytics / create, edit, archive |
| | View / manage contacts | Read contacts, segments, labels / create, edit, delete |
| | Manage sequences | Edit step content and spacing |
| | View analytics | Deliverability and engagement reports |
| | Use integrations | Push contacts and deals to connected tools, view and test automations |
| | Use AI | Remie and AI drafting, spending shared credits |
| **People** | Manage team | Invite, remove, re-role members |
| | Transfer ownership | Hand ownership to another member |
| **Sending** | Manage mailboxes | Connect, disconnect, configure senders |
| | Send campaigns | Start, pause, resume |
| | Use unified inbox | Read and reply from the shared inbox |
| **Workspace** | Manage settings | Workspace-wide settings, connecting integrations, building automations |
| | Manage billing | Checkout, invoices, plan changes and cancellation |
| | Manage API keys | Create and revoke workspace keys |

How the seeded roles map: **Viewer** holds only the two view permissions plus analytics. **Manager** adds everything operational (manage campaigns, contacts, sequences, mailboxes, send, unibox, integrations, AI). **Admin** adds team, settings, billing, and API keys. **Owner** is Admin plus ownership transfer.

A role also bounds what its members hand to software. An API key a member creates, and an app a member authorizes over OAuth, only gets the [API permissions their role covers](/api/permissions/#what-a-role-can-delegate), and an app's access shrinks with the member's role if it changes later. Members with **Manage API keys** or **Manage settings** can see every member's authorized apps under **Settings > OAuth apps > Authorized apps** and revoke any of them.

</Fold>

## Custom roles

On **Roles & access**, click **New role**: a name (up to 50 characters, and only "owner" is reserved), a color, and an optional description. Pick a template under **Start from** to copy a permission bundle, then toggle individual permissions. Assign it from the roster or the invite flow.

Five rules keep this safe:

- **Editing a role updates everyone assigned to it immediately.** The editor shows how many members are affected before you save.
- **You can only grant or take away permissions you hold**, so nobody can mint a role stronger than their own. Changing a role's permissions, deleting a role, re-roling a member and removing a member all need every permission involved, old and new; the owner holds them all.
- **Ownership transfer can never be part of a custom role.**
- **Members can hold several roles**, and their effective access is the union of all of them. A member left with no roles keeps membership but has no permissions.
- Up to 25 roles per workspace, including the seeded ones.

Custom roles apply everywhere permissions do: API access checks, dashboard visibility, and which realtime events a member receives. For a member with a restricted [access scope](#access-scope), only a role's read permissions take effect.
