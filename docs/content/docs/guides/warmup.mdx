Warmup sends ordinary-looking plain-text threads between participating mailboxes and records where they land. It reveals configuration and delivery problems, but it does not guarantee inbox placement.

![A mailbox's Warmup tab: participation, today's sends against the ramp target, pool standing and the ramp settings](/guides/mailbox-warmup.webp)

## Start warmup on a mailbox

<Steps>

<Step>

Open a mailbox from the Accounts list and go to the **Warmup** tab of its drawer.

</Step>

<Step>

Start warmup to turn on diagnostic sending.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose where warmup mail is filed: **Its own folder** (the default), **Leave it in the inbox**, or **Archive it**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pause to stop both sending and receiving warmup mail, or Stop to also turn diagnostic participation off, both while keeping the ramp history. Stop also lifts the [cold sending ceiling](#graduating-to-cold-sending); Pause keeps it.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- On Warmbly Cloud, every paid plan warms unlimited mailboxes in the premium pool. A new workspace warms up to 2 mailboxes in the free pool during its 14-day [free trial](/guides/billing/#the-free-trial), and a workspace that had the free plan before it closed to new workspaces keeps warming up to 10 there. Otherwise warmup needs a paid plan.
- A self-hosted instance warms every mailbox, but only against the other mailboxes on that instance, with no shared pool.
- New mailboxes start with warmup off. Once on, it starts at `10` emails a day by default and climbs by `+1` a day to a ceiling of `40`.
- Keep warmup on after campaigns start. A mailbox backing a live campaign drops to at most five warmup messages a day, and the pool replies to more of them.
- Warmup mail never appears in the unibox, and it is removed from the mailbox after 30 days by default.
- Spam placement is judged at Google, Microsoft and Yahoo only. It slows a mailbox down but does not, on its own, quarantine or block it.

## What warmup is and who gets it

Warmup sends natural plain-text threads between participating mailboxes. It measures observed placement and bounded actual-message DKIM evidence. It does not establish organic engagement or guarantee reputation improvement or inbox placement.

## Why it works

Diagnostics reveal configuration and delivery problems, not whether real recipients welcome outreach. Provider acceptance is not inbox placement; synthetic replies are not human feedback. Missing observations remain unknown.

## How it works

With warmup enabled, Warmbly repeatedly picks a partner mailbox (avoiding recent ones), decides whether to start a thread or reply to one, sends a short plaintext message through that mailbox's worker, then records stats and schedules the next send.

Warmup messages carry task/sender/recipient-bound application tokens. They use configured mailbox identities and no open/click pixels.

Providers may strip headers or replace identifiers. Exact authorized lineage and send-result identifiers correlate diagnostics; missing or ambiguous lineage stays closed. Tokens identify application-owned traffic, not cryptographic authentication.

When an SMTP relay rewrites a Message-ID, a valid recipient-bound verification token, the expected sender address and a confirmed send can authorize one received copy. Replies and message-specific DKIM verification use that copy's received identifier. Headerless arrivals still require an exact confirmed identifier for new diagnostic lineage; a matching subject or mailbox pair alone does not authorize a reply. Arrivals held before send confirmation or during a verification outage are retained and retried automatically while their token remains valid.

New mailboxes default off. Independent send/receive controls and shared daily/rolling limits are on the Warmup tab. Start/resume enables warmup sending and, when starting from Off or legacy, receiving with provider-supported spam rescue, reads, importance and stars or flags. Pause stops receiving as well as sending, and Stop disables both participation directions, each while retaining ramp history and holds. A mailbox whose warmup is paused or was never started receives no warmup mail, whatever its receive setting; the send and receive settings only narrow participation while warmup is on. Recipient-only (sending off, receiving on, warmup on) keeps receiving and cannot reply. Sender-only does not authorize recipient engagement. Existing receive-enabled diagnostic mailboxes also use these actions after the consumer, backend and workers are upgraded; no new setting or data migration is needed. Existing NULL stays legacy until explicit action; unrelated edits do not relabel it. Cloud controls require updated Cloud replicas. See [send safety](/development/send-safety/).

### When a send fails

The Warmup tab shows provider failures from the last 24 hours with their reported time when no later warmup send has been confirmed. The message expires even on an already-open page without another send. A lack of confirmed delivery is not proof that the mailbox is still failing. Provider and sign-in errors appear immediately; worker-loading hiccups do not.

A worker-loading warning appears only for an active, sending-enabled mailbox after repeated loading failures span at least an hour and the latest failure is less than an hour old. It expires when that evidence becomes stale, including on an already-open page, and is hidden when participation is paused or stopped. One historical failed attempt is never displayed indefinitely as an active loading problem. A later provider-confirmed warmup send clears it; synchronization or a dispatched task alone does not prove delivery.

"The sending worker has not loaded this mailbox yet" means that attempt did not send. It is not proof of a deliverability hold, a refused sign-in or an IP allowlist problem. Active mailboxes are reloaded automatically; after a worker restarts, it keeps reporting its restart until the backend acknowledges the request to reload its mailboxes.

Persistent loading failures and pending warmup tasks at least an hour past their intended schedule are checked every five minutes and reported to configured operator channels and error tracking. The latter is a dispatch problem, not evidence of rejected credentials. The local dispatcher persists a five-minute retry delay after internal failures so an old failing batch cannot indefinitely block newer tasks. Backoff does not alter the intended send time, clear a safety hold, or bypass provider limits. A send whose preparation was interrupted before it was handed to a worker (for example by a restart) is retired after ten minutes, so it never holds up the mailbox's next warmup email.

A missing mailbox also requests another reload through the worker heartbeat. Existing warmup sends stuck before execution recover automatically: after ten minutes, the scheduler retires a nonce-protected command only when neither worker execution nor a delivery receipt exists, refunds its reserved count, and resumes the mailbox's normal schedule. Delayed copies of the retired command cannot send. Customers do not need to disconnect or re-add their mailboxes. Actually started sends with missing outcomes, conflicting outcomes, provider cooldowns, authentication failures and other safety holds are not cleared by this repair.

Recovery runs at backend startup and every minute in bounded batches. The ten-minute cutoff is unchanged: it protects recent authorizations, not a waiting period added after each release. Existing older commands can be retired on the first pass; large fleets can need subsequent passes to reseed every schedule. Sending hours, quotas and actual provider failures still apply.

Kafka workers process independent mailbox lanes concurrently instead of making the whole worker wait for one command. Older queued commands are grouped by their mailbox payload too. The broker checkpoint never passes unfinished or failed work, and failed subscriptions reopen for replay. The default is eight lanes; see [worker configuration](/development/configuration/#workers).

Unacknowledged warmup filing actions remain durable. They retry every five minutes initially, then every fifteen minutes after twenty minutes of pending time, capped at every thirty minutes after an hour. Repeated arrivals do not reset that age. This reduces repeat commands during a worker backlog without discarding pending filing or counting it as a failed send.

If outbound admission refuses a warmup command before publication, a successful deferral returns it to the schedule without recording a failed send. A deferral whose database outcome is uncertain is not treated as proof of failure. Existing records affected by an older preparation-result race also recover automatically, but only when the recorded internal preparation failure predates the new authorization and there is no execution, outbound reservation, provider-confirmed send or receipt. Only that provably unstarted command's false conflict is released; genuine conflicting outcomes remain held.

Follow a provider-specific error when one is shown. Google and Microsoft sign-ins use their native APIs, not SMTP settings. Reconnect those mailboxes only for a reported sign-in or permission error. SMTP host, port, password and IP allowlist checks apply to SMTP mailboxes when the error reports a mail-server connection problem. Warmbly Cloud makes those connections from its own network, not your self-hosted instance's address.

Recovery does not bypass paused participation, provider limits or deliverability holds. If the warning persists, give support the mailbox address and the failure time so it can be matched to the worker's loading and sending records.

### Where the content comes from

Warmup mail reads like ordinary correspondence: a short, specific subject, an opening and up to five alternating replies between two people who know each other loosely. It never says it is automated, generated or a warmup, and any turn that does is refused before it sends. Topics, tone, structure and length vary from thread to thread, and replies keep the same subject and the real sender names.

Every instance ships with a hand-written library of about seventy threads, so warmup works with no AI provider at all. With an OpenAI key configured and `AI_PROVIDER` unset or set to `openai`, background batches add generated threads. Each passes structural and wording checks, then a separate review of the complete thread (TypeSafe when configured, otherwise OpenAI) before it can be used. An unavailable, incomplete or uncertain review never approves content.

For each new thread, Warmbly picks a conversation neither mailbox has seen in the last 30 days, as sender or recipient, preferring ones neither has ever seen and the least used. When a pair has seen everything recently, it reuses the one they saw longest ago instead of waiting, so a generation outage never pauses warmup. Duplicate subjects or openings are rejected across the library and every approved batch. Threads started with the retired "Simulated diagnostic" wording stop at their next reply, and the mailbox carries on with new content.

The generator refills the AI bank every six hours toward a target of at least 200 threads, rising with demand up to 5,000, in batches of at most 250 and within a default budget of 1,000 a day. Operators can adjust cadence, targets and budgets in the admin content controls; see [generation configuration](/development/warmup-generation/). None of this guarantees inbox placement or reputation improvement.

### Your campaign copy

Once a mailbox has warmed for 7 days, about a quarter of its new warmup threads open with the copy of a campaign it is sending for, so you see how the providers treat the copy your prospects actually get, and the pool's opens and spam rescues happen on that copy. There is nothing to set up or turn on. Nothing about it guarantees inbox placement or a better reputation.

Warmup uses a campaign's opening steps while the campaign is active: the first step, any step that starts its own thread, and each active A/B variant of those. Follow-ups that reply in the lead's thread are not used, since a warmup recipient has no thread with you. Each one is rendered for its warmup recipient exactly as the campaign renders it for a lead: merge fields, spintax, the HTML part (or plain text for a text-only campaign), the mailbox signature, the opt-out line, UTM tags and the campaign's open pixel and links on its tracking domain. The recipient's name comes from their mailbox name and the company from a business address's domain. A step that needs a value that recipient lacks is left for another recipient rather than guessed at. Opens and clicks on warmup mail never count in campaign analytics.

A step is never used when it has AI blocks or form links (written for each lead), uses a custom field or the lead's phone number, calls itself automated or a test, or fails the warmup content checks. Those mailboxes simply warm on ordinary conversations instead.

The copy goes only to business inboxes on Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, the kind of inbox cold email is written for, and never to a free Gmail, Outlook.com or Yahoo address or a smaller mail host. Of those, it goes only to pool mailboxes that file warmup mail out of the inbox (the default), never to one that leaves warmup where its owner reads it, so it is judged by a provider's filter rather than read by a stranger. A recipient is sent a given step once in 30 days, as a prospect would be, and a mailbox sends the same step or variant at most once a day, preferring the one it used longest ago.

The recipient then answers like a prospect: interested, not right now, not a fit, a question about how it would work, an offer of a short call, or already covered. Your mailbox answers that in kind. Each thread draws its own pair from a hand-written set, so threads differ from one another, and no line invents a fact about your copy.

Each email step of a campaign shows its **Warmup placement** under the step's A/B results: the inbox, tabs and spam rates for every arm over the last 30 days, and the inbox rate at Google Workspace and at Microsoft 365. An arm appears once 20 recipients have reported where it landed, and a provider once 10 have, so a handful of early receipts never reads as a rate. `GET /campaigns/:id/warmup-placement` returns the same figures.

## Pools

A pool is the set of mailboxes that warm each other. Your mailbox exchanges warmup mail within its own pool, with the mirrored crossings documented below. Every instance has both pools from install, so a self-hosted instance warms within its own pools from the first mailbox. Only an instance [linked to Warmbly Cloud](/guides/warmbly-cloud/) earlier joins the hosted one; Cloud links no new instances.

| Pool | Who is in it |
|------|--------------|
| `premium` | Paid organizations' mailboxes. The default for paid accounts, isolated from lower-trust traffic. |
| `free` | Mailboxes of workspaces in their free trial or that kept the earlier free plan, and lower-trust mailboxes, kept separate so free traffic does not mix into premium reputation. Other workspaces without a plan do not join it. |

A mailbox belongs to exactly one pool at a time. When its plan changes the mailbox moves between pools rather than joining a second one, and it takes its warmup standing with it, so a quarantine or block is not cleared by changing tier.

Premium access is to the safe partner set, not just to receivers with paid subscriptions. Its eligible recipients include free-tier mailboxes that have been pool members for at least three days, are healthy now, and belong to another workspace in good standing. These partners are available even when the premium tier is large. Both tiers are weighted by coverage, observed placement and exchange balance, without a paid-receiver-first preference. Every borrowed mailbox is checked against its own pool's standing before use. Mailboxes remain members of their original pool; this is a permitted exchange, not a pool merge.

The borrow is returned: a free-tier mailbox that meets the same bar (healthy now, a member for at least three days, in a workspace in good standing) may write to a paying mailbox that wrote to it in the last 14 days, and to no other paying mailbox. A visit is returned alongside the mailbox's own tier rather than after it, so a paying inbox is written back to even when the free tier is large. Nothing unsolicited from the free tier reaches a paying inbox: the paying side always opens the exchange, and a free mailbox on watch keeps warming in its own tier but stops calling on paying ones.

Every draw also favours the inbox that is owed the most. A mailbox that has sent to many partners and heard back from few is drawn up to four times as often as one in balance, and the preference fades as the pool pays it back, so traffic settles near parity instead of overshooting. The other way is bounded too: an inbox is sent at most about twice as many new threads a day as it sends (never fewer than 10, never more than 60), and once it reaches that it is left out of every draw for the rest of the day. Free-tier senders stop at three quarters of that, so the rest of every inbox's day is kept for premium mailboxes. Replies are outside this count, because each answers mail the inbox sent itself. A mailbox with nobody left to write to sends nothing and tries again later rather than mailing the same partner twice.

Partner/domain diversity broadens diagnostic coverage. It is not identity rotation to evade quotas or evidence of reputation efficacy.

A [placement seed inbox](/guides/placement-tests/#your-own-seed-inboxes) never joins a pool: marking a mailbox as a seed turns its warmup off, and starting warmup on a seed is refused, because a seed that exchanged mail with the pool would stop being a stranger to every sender in it. It never sends campaign mail either.

Self-hosted instances warm only among their own mailboxes. That local warmup works, but it is not effective: providers learn to recognise a few mailboxes that only write to each other, and one instance has nowhere near enough of them. For these pools of thousands of real mailboxes, use a hosted workspace on [app.warmbly.com](https://app.warmbly.com) and move the mailboxes there. Warmbly Cloud no longer links new self-hosted instances; one [linked earlier](/guides/warmbly-cloud/) keeps its enrolled mailboxes in these pools. On an instance that is not linked, the **Mailboxes** page says so in a banner, with **Upgrade on Warmbly Cloud** and **Community on Discord**, since Warmbly does not support self-hosted instances. **Upgrade on Warmbly Cloud** opens sign-up on app.warmbly.com (an existing account can sign in from the same page) and then the plan chooser. The banner also links to **Settings > Data**, where a [workspace export](/guides/workspace-export-import/) carries the workspace and, with **Include mailbox credentials** on, its mailboxes still connected.

### Choosing partners

Outside-workspace partners broaden observations; unlinked self-hosted instances may have only their own mailboxes. Diversity does not turn automated traffic into organic engagement.

Within that order, partners are picked by weight rather than at random. Three things move the weight:

- **Host and domain coverage.** Eligible mail hosts start with balanced total weight, so a host with many custom domains does not crowd out a consumer host sharing one domain, such as Gmail. Recently used domains are downweighted within each host; partner no-repeat rules and daily receiving caps still apply. Gmail and Google Workspace are distinct hosts, as are Outlook.com and Microsoft 365.
- **Where you are landing.** Warmbly tracks your spam-placement rate separately for each recipient mail host over the last seven days: who actually runs that recipient's mail (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho Mail, Hostinger and so on), read from the partner's mail servers rather than its address, so two custom domains on different hosts are judged apart. A mailbox landing in spam at one host but not the others is sent fewer partners on that host until the rate falls, without waiting for an overall health band to trip. A free Gmail or Outlook.com mailbox works the other way at Google, Microsoft and Yahoo: it is sent more partners where it lands in spam (up to twice as many), because the pool rescues its mail at the [recovery](#recovery-engagement) rate and that rescue is what repairs its standing with that provider. Other hosts still get fewer.
- **Your routing rules**, when you have configured any. A rule's weight multiplies a partner's chance; a weight of `0` is not a weight but an exclusion, so that pairing never happens.

Balanced starting weights are not a fixed sending ratio. Available recipients, health, daily caps, recent pairings, placement feedback and routing rules still determine the actual split. More traffic to a host does not establish improved inbox placement.

An exclusion is honoured even when it leaves nothing: if every available partner is excluded by your rules, the mailbox sends no warmup mail that tick and tries again later, rather than mailing someone you ruled out because the pool had nobody else. The same holds when a partner writes first: your mailbox does not reply to an address your rules exclude. Rules apply to paid-tier mailboxes.

A struggling host is downweighted, never excluded: a sender that stops mailing a host entirely can never discover that it recovered there. A host is only judged once at least five of your warmup emails have verifiably arrived there in the window, so one bad delivery out of two is treated as noise rather than a pattern. You can see the numbers this reads under **Deliverability > Warmup inbox placement**, by expanding a provider to its hosts.

### Mailboxes the pool cannot reach

A warmup email only counts once the recipient's own sync finds it. Every partner is also weighted by how much of the pool's warmup mail to it arrived over the last seven days, so a partner whose mail half arrives is drawn about half as often.

A partner that received at least `5` warmup emails from at least `2` different workspaces in that week, and verifiably received none of them, is not offered to anyone. That is a mailbox whose domain has no working mail server, an address that no longer exists, or a server that refuses the mail, and every send to it would only come back to you as a bounce. Emails sent in the last 6 hours are not counted yet, so a slow sync is never mistaken for a dead inbox, and losses from a single workspace never take a partner out, so one sender's problem is not blamed on the recipient.

Nothing is permanent about it. One warmup email that arrives brings the mailbox straight back, and as the week's evidence ages out it is offered again on its own. If it is your mailbox, the [Advisor](/guides/advisor/) says so and what to check, starting with the domain's MX records.

### Seeing the diversity you are getting

The drawer shows confirmed partners/domains/workspaces and verified arrivals over seven days, locally or through Cloud. Pending and failed attempts do not count as confirmed sending. Narrow coverage is a diagnostic limitation, not a reputation verdict.

The **Analytics** tab of the same drawer charts the exchange over time: sent, received and replies per day, with the total received against the total sent for the range. The same numbers are in the [warmup analytics](/api/reference/analytics/#get-warmup-analytics) endpoint.

Pause and Stop both end receiving: no new warmup mail or reply is sent to the mailbox, and unstarted sends already queued for it are withdrawn. Mail a provider accepted before then can still arrive. The mailbox keeps its pool standing. Turning warmup back on resumes sending, and resumes receiving only when the mailbox's participation setting allows it. Lost authority, disconnection, quarantine or block also restrict participation. Health alone is not consent.

## Seeing where warmup lands

Every warmup email a mailbox sends is looked for in the partner's mailbox, and when it arrives Warmbly records where: the primary inbox, a Gmail category tab (Promotions, Updates, Social or Forums), or spam. Nothing is estimated or sampled, and the history is kept for the life of the mailbox rather than only the latest day.

| Where | What it shows |
|-------|---------------|
| **Inbox** column in the Accounts list | The mailbox's inbox rate over the last seven days, coloured by band. Click it to open the breakdown |
| **Deliverability** tab in the mailbox drawer | The same rate with its counts, then day-by-day inbox, tabs and spam, the rate over time, and the split per recipient provider, for `7d` to `90d` |
| **Deliverability > Warmup inbox placement** | The workspace total over time and per provider, and every mailbox ranked worst first |

The **inbox rate** is inbox plus category tabs over everything delivered in the last seven days: mail in Promotions was delivered and not filtered, which is what the number is asked to say. The tabs are still counted separately so you can see them.

| Inbox rate | Band |
|------------|------|
| `90%` or more | Healthy |
| `80%` to `90%` | Watch |
| Below `80%` | Landing in spam |

The rate is taken at Google, Microsoft and Yahoo only, the providers that judge a mailbox. Smaller mail hosts run their own filters, so their spam folders never count toward it: a mailbox whose warmup mail only reached small hosts shows no rate rather than one built from them. Other mail hosts are still shown: their inbox rate sits right under the headline in the drawer and on **Deliverability**, and they appear in the daily charts, the counts and the per-provider breakdown. It only appears once `20` deliveries at those three are in the window, the same sample the pool needs before it acts on spam placement. Until then the mailbox shows how far along it is (`8/20`) rather than a percentage that would swing on every delivery. The mailbox's health score is capped at its inbox rate, so a mailbox with some mail in spam reads `97`, not a flat `100`.

Filter by provider to distinguish observations from gaps. Confirmed out of spam counts the rescues the recipient's mailbox reported done, beside how many were requested, how many found the message already gone, how many the provider refused and how many have no outcome reported (rescues from before outcomes were recorded stay in that last group). A move is not proof of a reputation benefit. Only mail that arrived in spam is rescued. Receiving-authorized warmup can request spam rescue, but placement remains the originally observed folder, not a rewritten success after a move. Unconfirmed means not observed after timeout, not verified failure. First-folder evidence is separate from housekeeping; absent historical folders stay unknown. Days are UTC. See [warmup placement](/api/reference/analytics/#get-warmup-placement).

## The ramp

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Start volume | `10`/day | Warmup emails on day one |
| Daily increase | `+1`/day | How much the target grows daily |
| Ceiling | `40`/day (`25`/day on a free Gmail or Outlook.com account) | Where the ramp stops climbing |

Configured diagnostic targets start at 10/day and rise by one toward 40/day before other constraints. These are product pacing defaults, not universal safe volumes or readiness evidence.

On a self-hosted instance, a mailbox warmed by Warmbly Cloud runs the ramp, hours and days Warmbly Cloud holds for it. The Warmup tab edits those through the cloud, and hides the content segment, placement, folder and retention, which only apply to warmup sent from your instance. See [warmup settings](/guides/warmbly-cloud/#warmup-settings).

Four things shape the real daily number: sends are spread across your warmup hours with jitter, the target is capped by how many eligible partners exist, a mailbox whose health drops gets reduced volume and wider spacing until it recovers, and a recent spam placement holds the ramp where it is.

The partner cap exists because a mailbox never writes to the same partner twice in a day. When it applies, the **Warmup** tab of the mailbox drawer says so: it shows the ramp's number next to how many partners are available to the mailbox today, and the target it reports is the lower of the two. The count can fall during the day as partners reach their daily limit, and it recovers the next day. The same tab names the pool the mailbox warms in.

Warmup hours are read in the mailbox's timezone: its own when one is set on the **Settings** tab of its drawer, otherwise the workspace timezone under **Settings > Profile > Timezones**, and UTC when neither is set. Set the workspace timezone once and every mailbox that follows it reads its warmup hours in the team's day. A mailbox with its own timezone keeps it, and changing the workspace timezone does not change any mailbox's hours. The day's warmup count also resets at midnight in that timezone. The queued send in the mailbox list and the Warmup tab is shown on the same clock, with the zone named when it differs from your browser's, so a mailbox on London hours shows `09:12 GMT` rather than the same moment in your own time.

Choose **Sending days** on the **Warmup** tab. **Every day** explicitly includes Saturday and Sunday; **Weekdays** selects Monday to Friday. You can also select individual days. Every selected day applies to warmup, even when the sending plan's working days exclude it. This does not change campaign working days.

**Follow sending plan** uses the working days on the **Sending** tab, which default to Monday to Friday. With **Fixed schedule**, it means every day. Existing mailboxes with no selected warmup days keep this inherited schedule. The day picker shows the days that apply; selecting a day switches to an explicit warmup schedule.

While [Sending behavior](/guides/sending-behavior/) is on, warmup uses the overlap between its configured hours and the day's varied workday, including the break. A warmup window starting at 08:00 still waits until 09:15 if that day's workday opens then. On an explicitly selected weekend day, warmup rolls the same hours and break without opening that day for campaigns. The Warmup tab shows the workday's start and end ranges and warns when the hours cannot overlap. Pending sends wait and recheck after five minutes until the settings allow sending. Switch to **Fixed schedule** on the **Sending** tab to use the warmup hours alone.

Changing warmup hours, days or timezone, or switching sending behavior on or off, moves the queued warmup send to the new schedule as soon as you save. To recalculate an existing queued time, choose its day preset again and save. The mailbox list refreshes its queued time and shows whether warmup follows the sending plan or an explicit set of days. **Start warmup** for several mailboxes offers the same day presets; **Follow sending plan** resolves separately for each locally warmed mailbox and is hidden when the selection includes a Cloud-linked mailbox. Volume, consent, health and provider restrictions still apply on every selected day.

Bulk start waits until mailbox ownership is confirmed. If ownership refreshes while the dialog is open, your settings stay visible and new submissions wait for the check to finish. An operation already in progress finishes for its original mailbox selection and leaves any other selected mailboxes alone. Mailboxes that fail to start remain selected for retry.

Warmup hours must end later than they start on the same day. Equal or reversed hours are rejected, including a change to only one endpoint that would invalidate the stored window. Existing invalid windows are not widened or corrected automatically: pending sends stay unstarted and recheck after five minutes, while admin monitoring shows **Invalid warmup sending hours** until the configured window is corrected. Other send protections still apply after correction; this does not replay uncertain sends or confirm mailbox recovery.

### Holding the ramp on an early signal

If any warmup email lands in the spam folder of a Google, Microsoft or Yahoo recipient, that mailbox stops climbing immediately:

- today's target is cut by about a quarter, for 48 hours
- the daily increase pauses for three days
- the paused days are subtracted rather than made up, so the ramp does not jump three steps in one morning when it resumes

Another placement during a pause extends it and does not lift the ramp: repeated spam placement can only ever slow a mailbox down.

A landing at any other mail host never holds the ramp. Those hosts each run their own filter, so their spam folder says more about the server than about your mailbox; see [how spam placement is judged](#how-spam-placement-is-judged).

Nothing needs to be switched on and no health band has to trip first. That matters because the bands need a sample before they can judge a mailbox at all (twenty verified warmup deliveries in seven days, a hundred delivered in thirty), which a mailbox in its first fortnight has not reached. Without this, the mailbox landing in spam on day three would keep adding an email a day until it had sent enough to be judged.

Placement slowdown and ramp pause have different windows. The drawer shows observed targets/holds. Lifecycle actions retain ramp history and restrictive holds rather than erasing negative evidence.

<Callout type="info" title="Warmup and campaigns coexist">
  Keep warmup on after campaigns start. A mailbox backing a live campaign drops to at most five warmup messages per day, leaving the campaign as primary traffic, and gets [recovery engagement](#recovery-engagement) on them, so it keeps receiving replies while most cold recipients do not answer. Five a day is also what keeps its [inbox placement](#pool-safety) measured, which needs 20 delivered warmup emails in seven days. When no live campaign uses the mailbox, warmup returns to its own ramp. Warmup does not make repetitive or unwanted copy safe: you still need real targeting, personalization, suppression, and unsubscribe support.
</Callout>

## Replies

Replies continue the exact pinned thread and subject, each parent is answered once, and exhausted, retired or missing threads close.

Replies thread properly with a real `Re:` subject and `In-Reply-To` header, and candidates must be between 45 minutes and seven days old, so nothing is answered instantly or revived indefinitely.

The **Reply rate** setting targets the share of a mailbox's own warmup sends that are replies over its last seven UTC days. With enough eligible received mail, a mailbox set to `30%` approaches three replies for every ten warmup emails. The mailbox's **Reply target** card and the Analytics tab's **Replies** card both show the measured share over those same seven days, next to the target, and update as the mailbox sends and receives warmup mail. The reply count on the Analytics tab covers the last 30 days. Replies never add volume: each one takes the place of a send the mailbox was going to make anyway. `0%` turns replies off.

Replies reach that share two ways:

- **On arrival.** When a verified warmup email arrives, Warmbly includes the next send in its target calculation. If that leaves room for a reply, it sometimes points the next scheduled send back at whoever wrote, 45 minutes to 5 hours later and inside its own warmup hours. The chance is the mailbox's reply rate (raised for a sender in [recovery](#recovery-engagement)), drawn once when the reply is scheduled rather than again when it sends. Each mailbox has one warmup send queued at a time, so a reply-back moves that send earlier and aims it, and never pushes a send the mailbox had already planned sooner. If that send is due within 45 minutes of the arrival, or is already answering someone, the arrival is not answered then.
- **When a send goes out.** Warmbly compares the mailbox's reply share over the last seven UTC days with its target and turns the difference into a reply chance for that send: the target itself when on track, more when behind and less when ahead, never more than one and a half times the target or less than half of it. For example, a `30%` target with no replies yet gives a `45%` chance, and one already replying to `80%` of its sends gives `15%`. A shortfall is made up over several days rather than in one burst of replies, and a mailbox ahead of its target keeps answering its conversations. A selected reply answers mail at least 45 minutes and at most seven days old that the mailbox has not answered. Mail from senders in [recovery](#recovery-engagement) is considered first, then replies in conversations already under way, deepest first, so replies build back-and-forth threads instead of answering many threads once; newest first breaks ties.

Together these help the measured rate converge on the setting, including for busy mailboxes whose next send is almost always less than 45 minutes away. Small send counts and a recent setting change can make it differ temporarily. The rate can fall short when eligible received mail is unavailable, threads have reached their message caps, or the partners that wrote to it can no longer receive. Reply selection proceeds in batches with recovery priority, continuing past messages with unavailable content or partners until it finds a usable reply or exhausts the candidates.

A reply is not held to the recipient's daily receiving cap and does not count against it. The cap limits new threads into an inbox; a reply answers mail that inbox sent, so its own sending already bounds how many replies it can get, and a free mailbox's replies never take the share of a paying inbox's day that is kept for premium senders. Replies stop before a thread reaches its message cap so replies cannot answer replies indefinitely. A reply crosses the free and paid tiers like the mail it answers: a paid mailbox answers a free partner that wrote to it, and a free mailbox answers a paid one while it is healthy and its workspace is in good standing.

Scheduling respects windows, provider feedback, shared limits and spacing. Jitter distributes load, not human identities. Receiving participation allows probabilistic spam rescue, reads, importance and stars or flags on verified warmup messages. Filing and selected spam rescues are requested immediately; reads, importance and stars are durably scheduled after a randomized dwell. Current receiving permission and worker assignment are checked again at execution time. These actions are automation, not human feedback or a guaranteed reputation improvement.

</Fold>

## Keeping warmup out of your inbox

Warmup is real mail in a real mailbox, so there are two separate places it could show up: the Warmbly [unibox](/guides/unibox/), and your own mail client. Both are handled, and they are handled differently.

### In your mail client

Warmup mail is filed out of the way in the mailbox itself, in **both directions**: the messages this mailbox received, and the copy of every warmup message it sent. That second half matters more than it sounds, because a mailbox warming at forty a day would otherwise bury a week of real sent mail under machine traffic.

The filing is per mailbox, on the **Warmup** tab of its drawer:

| Setting | What your mail client shows |
|---------|------------------------------|
| **Its own folder** (default) | One folder, `Warmbly` unless you rename it, holding every warmup message in and out. Nothing warmup-related in Inbox or Sent. |
| **Leave it in the inbox** | Warmup stays where the provider placed it unless selected for spam rescue into Inbox. |
| **Archive it** | Out of the inbox with no folder of its own: findable by search, listed in All Mail on Gmail. |

The folder is created the first time it is needed, and it is also the answer to "what is this message?": nothing but warmup is filed there, so the folder itself is what identifies warmup traffic in a mail client. If another tool already made a folder you use for this, name that one instead and warmup joins it rather than adding a second.

Spam rescue is provider-specific: Gmail removes Spam and supports Important and Starred labels; Microsoft moves verified Junk messages to Inbox and supports high importance and a follow-up flag; IMAP moves messages out of recognized Spam/Junk folders and uses flags plus best-effort not-junk keywords. After a rescue runs, the mailbox reports whether the message moved, was already out of spam, was no longer there or was refused, and that is what the placement history shows. IMAP does not have a universal Gmail-style Important label, and SMTP-only mailboxes cannot perform recipient-side actions without mailbox access. A move is not proof that a provider learned a positive reputation signal. Filing remains housekeeping, not engagement evidence. Historical NULL/legacy retains existing behavior until explicit action. Upgrade the consumer, backend and workers together; previously skipped engagement is not replayed onto old mail.

On a mailbox connected with **Sign in with Google**, or through a [whole-domain grant](/development/whole-domain-connect/#optional-scope-for-warmup-engagement-over-imap) whose delegation entry lists the optional `https://mail.google.com/` scope, the recipient-side actions (taking a message out of Spam, marking it read, starring it and marking it important) run over IMAP at `imap.gmail.com`, signed in with the mailbox's own Google token. A rescued message moves out of Spam to where the filing setting puts warmup: the inbox, the warmup label, or All Mail for **Archive it**. Starring sets Gmail's star, and marking important adds the Important label. Filing, retention deletes and removal checks still run on the Gmail API. The worker opens one IMAP session per mailbox at a time and closes it after a short idle period. A mailbox whose token lacks full mail access (a sign-in mailbox connected before Warmbly asked for it, or a delegation entry without the optional scope), or whose Workspace administrator has turned IMAP off, runs the same actions on the Gmail API instead. [Re-authorizing](/guides/mailboxes/#reconnecting-an-account) a Google sign-in mailbox asks for full mail access and moves its warmup actions to IMAP.

Mail that arrives somewhere unexpected is still handled. A server-side rule, or another warmup tool's filter, can put warmup mail in a folder of its own choosing before Warmbly sees it; the message is found there and filed from there, and one already sitting in the destination is left alone rather than moved onto itself.

Filing happens as each message arrives, so changing the setting applies to warmup from that point on.

If filing fails, it is not reported as completed. An updated worker can release that filing command for later retry only after the backend confirms that the exact filing is still in its durable recovery queue. Recovery retries use the current worker assignment and folder setting, spaced by 5, 15 or 30 minutes as the queued filing ages. Unknown recovery authority keeps the existing command retry behavior. This does not resend mail, clear uncertain-send protection, acknowledge mailbox arrivals, or discard retention deletes and removal checks.

A sweep identifies missed application-owned diagnostics and applies authorized housekeeping. It does not establish retroactive authentication or engagement. Leaving mail in the inbox remains an owner choice.

The sweep repairs what it can still see, so it does not clear a backlog that built up before the setting existed. To do that on Gmail, search `label:Warmbly` and archive the results: warmup mail has always carried the label, so that selects exactly it. On Outlook and IMAP the received mail is already in the folder, and only the Sent copies from before this change stay in Sent.

### Retention

Authorized retention deletes old diagnostic mail after its window, 30 days by default. Gmail moves it to Trash; Outlook, Microsoft 365 and IMAP delete it. Stored copies follow retention policy. Recipient deletion is not tampering or proof of failure.

Set warmup_retention_days on the Warmup tab or API. Empty follows the instance default; the minimum is three days. Self-host operators configure Instance > Configuration > Data retention. See [data control](/development/data-control/#warmup-mail-in-mailboxes).

At the volumes warmup runs, a few plaintext messages of a couple of kilobytes each, a mailbox in balance takes years to fill even on a small hosting quota, so the retention is there so that you never have to think about it rather than because the folder is about to overflow. You do not need to clear the folder by hand, and if you do, see [pool safety](#pool-safety) for what counts.

<Callout type="info" title="Gmail and the Sent copy">
  Gmail exposes folders as labels and does not document whether `SENT` may be removed. Warmbly asks once per mailbox: where Gmail allows it, the sent copy leaves Sent entirely; where it refuses, the copy keeps its place in Sent and carries the warmup label, and nothing asks again. Outlook, Microsoft 365 and IMAP mailboxes move the copy out of Sent normally. SMTP mailboxes never get one in the first place, because Warmbly does not file a Sent copy for warmup.
</Callout>

### In the unibox

Mailbox-sync recovery replays stored arrivals, not sends. Recognized warmup remains hidden, and replay does not create another warmup send or count sync progress as proof that a provider sent mail. Receipt verification and duplicate-send protections remain separate from the mailbox's live-sync status. See [mailbox sync recovery and its limits](/guides/mailboxes/#what-gets-synced).

Warmup never appears in the unified inbox, in either direction. Warmbly recognizes it by its token, including for recipient-only mailboxes. A consumed or expired token still identifies warmup for filtering, but cannot trigger engagement again. When the header is missing, a recorded message identifier can identify older warmup during history sync; the sender-and-subject fallback stays limited to recent inbound sends so an ordinary conversation is not hidden just because it shares a subject. Existing verified warmup entries are removed from the unibox automatically in background batches, without deleting mail at the provider or changing warmup standing.

Manual replies to verified diagnostic parents can stay hidden from the unibox and follow housekeeping. They do not authorize another automated turn or count as real campaign replies.

If a provider syncs a headerless Sent copy before reporting its final message identifier, Warmbly holds a possible match for verification. The send confirmation then identifies which message was warmup, and ordinary mail sharing its subject can enter the unibox. A matching subject alone never permanently hides a sent message.

Bounce notices and spam reports about warmup are held back too. A warmup send that hard-bounces produces an ordinary "Undelivered Mail Returned to Sender" from the receiving server, which carries no warmup token of its own; it names a pool partner nobody chose to write to, so it is recognized by the send it is about and kept out of the unibox. The same reasoning keeps it out of your deliverability record: a warmup bounce never suppresses an address in your suppression list and never counts toward the bounce rate your campaigns are judged on. Warmup bounce rate is a band in [pool safety](#pool-safety) instead. A bounce or complaint about a **campaign** send is unaffected and still appears, because that is real outreach reaching a real recipient.

### Nowhere else either

Warmup writes to none of the tables the customer-facing views read, so nothing needs to filter it out after the fact:

- a contact's **Activity** timeline is built from campaign progress, opens, clicks, replies, notes, meetings and page hits, and warmup creates no contact and no campaign progress
- a contact's sent-mail list is keyed on campaign tasks, which a warmup send never has
- direct-mail analytics counts a mailbox's own sent and received mail and excludes every task-backed message, campaign and warmup alike
- notifications are raised for domain authentication, worker downtime and campaign guardrails, never per message

## Pool safety

Pools enforce permission and restrictive complaint/bounce/authentication evidence, current authority and durable holds. Deleting diagnostics or choosing spam is recipient withdrawal, not automatic tampering misconduct.

| State | Meaning | Effect |
|-------|---------|--------|
| `healthy` | Normal | Full volume |
| `watch` | Mild concern | Warmup at full volume with recovery engagement; cold volume eased |
| `throttled` | Probation | Volume cut, spacing widened, recovery engagement until recovery |
| `quarantined` | Clear problem | Out of the shared pool for a cooldown |
| `blocked` | Serious or repeated abuse | Out for longer, review required |

The thresholds for each signal are in the [health bands](/guides/deliverability/#health-bands) table.

Watch and throttled standing are warnings, not connection failures. They do not subtract a fixed number of points from mailbox health: the score is still capped by the measured inbox rate, and separate account errors can lower it further. The score is overall health out of 100, not another inbox percentage.

### How spam placement is judged

Spam placement is a folder outcome, not misconduct or evidence of diagnostic reputation benefit. It slows sending but does not alone quarantine or block a recipient:

- `10%` puts the mailbox on watch: warmup keeps its volume and pace (its ramp holds while spam landings are recent), the pool engages harder with its mail (see [recovery engagement](#recovery-engagement)), and cold volume is eased to `70%`.
- `20%` throttles it: warmup keeps running at half volume with double spacing, and cold volume is halved.
- A throttle steps down to watch below `15%`, then returns to healthy below `7.5%`, on its own. They lift below the line that set them rather than at it, so a mailbox sitting near a line is not flipped in and out of a band (and announced to your integrations) with every delivery. The separate [early-signal ramp pause](#holding-the-ramp-on-an-early-signal) still applies to fresh spam landings.

### Recovery engagement

While a mailbox is on watch or throttled, the pool works harder for it instead of only slowing it down. Recipients that take part in engagement move nearly every copy of its warmup mail that lands in spam back out of spam (each mailbox at its own rate, so none does it every time) and mark more of it important, and its mail is answered first: a recipient below its own reply share answers it on arrival with at least a `60%` chance, and picks it before any other mail when a send comes due as a reply (unless the recipient's owner turned warmup replies off). Every recipient still holds its own reply share, so recovery decides who is answered, not how much. Replies take the place of the recipient's next scheduled warmup send, so the extra engagement adds no volume. It stops on its own when the band lifts.

A free Gmail (`gmail.com`, `googlemail.com`) or Outlook.com (`outlook.com`, `hotmail.com`, `live.com` and the other Microsoft consumer domains) mailbox gets recovery engagement all the time, whatever its standing. A personal account starts with far less sender trust than a Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 mailbox on your own domain, so the pool rescues, marks important and answers its mail at the recovery rates from the first day. Workspace and Microsoft 365 mailboxes get it while on watch or throttled, and while they back a live campaign: cold mail draws few replies, so the pool answers the reduced warmup those mailboxes still send ahead of other warmup mail. Those replies never count as campaign replies and never raise campaign volume.

Placement uses today and the preceding six UTC dates, matching the seven-day inbox rate on Deliverability and Emails. Older placement results stop affecting volume at the same boundary as the displayed rate. Complaints and mailbox tampering keep their separate rolling windows.

Only Google, Microsoft and Yahoo recipients judge a mailbox. They filter on sender reputation, which is what your cold email is judged on at those same providers. Every other mail host runs its own filter, often a stock rule set tuned for that one server, so a message landing in its spam folder is not evidence that the mail is spam. Those landings are shown in the placement view and never held against the mailbox, in the bands, the ramp or the [Advisor](/guides/advisor/). The rate is taken over verified deliveries at the three providers in the last seven days, and needs `20` of them before it acts.

So a mailbox that inboxes everything at Google and Microsoft stays healthy however many small hosts junk its mail. The reason in the mailbox drawer says what was judged: for example `25% of warmup mail delivered at Google, Microsoft and Yahoo landed in spam over 7 days (40 delivered). Other mail hosts (50% of 20) run their own filters and are not counted.`

Selection uses observed coverage to reduce uninformative pairings. Measurement allocation does not prove traffic to a host builds reputation.

Quarantined and blocked mailboxes are selected as neither sender nor recipient. Mail arriving in a mailbox is never held against it: every warmup message carries a single-use token bound to its recipient, so a token cannot be replayed or redirected, and one that lands where it does not belong is simply filed as ordinary mail.

Withdrawal creates no unconditional deletion/spam tampering strike. Owner-attributed spam can still create sender complaint evidence and suppression; provider-attributed moves remain placement evidence. Ambiguity charges nobody. Historical evidence is rechecked without resetting unrelated restrictive holds; failed searches stay unknown.

<Callout type="warn" title="Acting early protects everyone">
  Complaint, bounce and authentication evidence may hold sending. Placement slows pacing. Diagnostic age and automated engagement do not establish recovery.
</Callout>

Getting back in requires requalifying, not just waiting: healthy authentication, no recent complaints or hard-bounce spikes, and spam placement back to a low level. Return is gradual, not a jump back to the old ceiling.

A quarantine or a block also holds for its full term. The signals behind it age out of their windows long before it ends, and that does not release it early; a more serious finding can still replace it. A throttle is different and lifts as soon as the mailbox recovers, as the table says. When support lifts a pause or block, or approves an appeal against one, the strikes behind it are cleared with it, so the next evaluation does not reimpose it.

On a self-hosted instance linked to [Warmbly Cloud](/guides/warmbly-cloud/#safety-and-enforcement), a mailbox the cloud warms is judged there, and the instance applies the cloud's standing to its own campaigns with the same effects as this table.

The standing follows the address, not the connection. Removing a mailbox from the workspace and adding it back, or dropping out of the pool and rejoining, does not clear its score or its block: the same address in the same workspace rejoins with the standing it left with. That standing is kept for 90 days after the block ends or after the mailbox was removed, whichever is later; a block that requires review never lapses. A mailbox in good standing leaves nothing behind.

</Fold>

## Graduating to cold sending

Diagnostic-history mailboxes start cold sending at a conservative ceiling of five/day. Synthetic age never increases it. Recent confirmed human replies to actual campaign recipients permit bounded increases, subject to calendar days and negative feedback. Missing evidence leaves readiness/full-cap dates unknown. This product policy is not a provider-approved safe volume. The ceiling applies while warmup is running or paused. Stopping warmup removes it, leaving the mailbox's own daily cap and minimum gap, and starting warmup again restores it from the recent reply evidence. See [easing out of warmup](/guides/campaigns/#easing-out-of-warmup).

</Fold>

## Recommended posture

- Start new mailboxes around `10`/day and let the ramp climb slowly.
- Do not raise the ceiling above `40` casually.
- Keep warmup running alongside campaigns, not only before them.
- Prefer more healthy mailboxes over pushing a few too hard.
- Watch complaints and spam placement, not open-rate vanity metrics.
